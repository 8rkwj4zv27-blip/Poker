### What the family should demonstrate

- a large button legend;
- a panel or cabinet heading;
- a small instrument label;
- a money or statistic label;
- a warning/destructive label;
- disabled or unavailable wording where it helps judge ink and contrast.

Show a faithful production-derived baseline first, followed by a small number of genuinely different treatments. Keep the physical carrier, dimensions, surrounding frame and sample wording consistent so the owner is judging the printing system rather than a new component design.

### Required visual behaviour

- The text must read as ink, paint, engraving or a fixed plaque attached to a physical part.
- It must not glow, flicker, blink, scroll or inherit CRT effects.
- Use the established pixel type language; do not return to serif or beige editorial styling.
- Establish a clear hierarchy for large legends, section headings, small labels and semantic labels.
- Retain legibility at the narrow mobile viewport and across all four production themes.
- Apply semantic gold or coral/red only when the label genuinely means money/reward or danger/destruction. Ordinary labels should remain neutral or use restrained machine-information colour.

## Working method learned from the Lab

- Change one design variable at a time.
- Begin with a recognisable production baseline.
- Offer a few materially different choices, not many cosmetic permutations.
- Judge effects on the same construction and construction on the same content.
- Remove rejected explorations from the active Lab instead of accumulating an archive on the page.
- Keep durable decisions in documentation rather than relying on browser local storage.
- Stop at the requested decision; do not pre-empt the next family or migration.
