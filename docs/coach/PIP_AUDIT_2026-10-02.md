# P.I.P. abilities audit and proposed project plan

Date: 2 October 2026. Assessed build: v0.58.2-dev on
`codex/pip-brain-v2`, at `7e3355c`. This is the local development branch;
its behaviour must not be assumed to be installed on the owner's phone.

This report records an assessment and proposed work. It does not authorise
or claim implementation of the roadmap. No runtime code changed for the audit.

## Assessment

P.I.P. is a useful beginner coach with a strong observational foundation and
an increasingly capable tactical policy. He is not yet a complete poker
coach. His principal weakness is that the advice, action judgement, wording
and hand verdict are not held together by one durable decision record.
Some judgement rules also inspect the suggested bet rather than the bet
actually made. More lessons or more personalised memory would amplify those
problems until they are fixed.

The next milestone should be a reliability and language pass, followed by
deeper tactical reasoning, the hand breakdown, practice lessons and then
persistent observations of the player's habits.

## How he currently works

1. **Observes a decision.** `spot()` snapshots the player's cards, board,
   seat, pot, price, stacks, public actions and public opponent statistics.
   Hidden opponent cards are read only when publicly revealed at showdown.
2. **Estimates likely opponent hands.** Before the flop he uses starting-hand
   rankings and seat-based ranges. After the flop those ranges are weighted
   by public checks, bets, raises and calls, plus broad opponent habits.
3. **Estimates equity.** The existing poker maths simulates possible hands
   and remaining cards: 1,200 samples before the flop and 1,500 afterwards.
   These percentages are estimates conditioned on his assumed ranges.
4. **Judges actions.** Separate policies cover preflop, facing a postflop bet
   and being checked to. Verdicts are good/fine/mistake, with
   clear/leans/close confidence. They are heuristic classifications, not
   measured differences in expected chip profit.
5. **Chooses advice.** `advise()` judges fold/check, call and one proposed
   aggressive amount. It follows the judge's preferred action, or the
   best-rated available action. It does not compare every possible size.
6. **Plans a value bet.** The recent pass generates several candidate sizes,
   then chooses one using board texture, hand category, opponent habit and
   effective chips. The other sizes are listed but not scored for return.
7. **Speaks.** `coach-talk.js` selects authored wording from `coach-lines.js`,
   fills in numbers and routes it through HELP/TALK preferences. There is no
   language-model call or free conversation in this implementation.
8. **Records and reviews.** Each action stores the spot, choice and judgement.
   At the result, P.I.P. selects one notable decision using severity, street
   and pot size, then gives a reason and sometimes a lesson.
9. **Handles taps and vocabulary.** Taps progress through a read, lesson and
   tip. Vocabulary lessons already persist under `pip.coach`; the last 50
   hand records are session-only. Persistent player-habit coaching is absent.

## What is already useful

- Public-information boundaries and use of the real game maths are good foundations.
- Seat-aware preflop advice, price-aware calls, draw recognition and broad
  opponent habits are implemented.
- Value betting now distinguishes quiet boards, connected/suited boards,
  habitual callers and shallow commitment situations.
- Conservative draw-all-in checks address the previously reported bust advice.
- P.I.P. already distinguishes a bad outcome from some good decisions and
  uses shorter comfort after a particularly painful loss.
- HELP/TALK controls, progressive vocabulary, tap-for-more, wording rotation
  and limited immediate praise support a restrained teaching experience.
- Pot settlement uses the engine's actual pot splitting; the coach does not
  change poker outcomes.
- The isolated Brain Lab and pure-function sandbox make focused regressions possible.

## Confirmed problems and oversights

### A01 — Advice and judgement can still contradict each other (high)

The commitment-specific result line fixed one path, but ordinary advised
value bets still route to `bet.value.why.foldwin`, which can recommend a
smaller bet after P.I.P. advised the exact amount used.

Reproduced: Queens on Q-7-2, pot 110, advice bet 40. Acting for 40 produces
`bet.value`; a fold win can select the smaller-bet sentence. A fold alone
does not prove the chosen size was wrong, and the folded cards are unknown.

There is another short-opponent variant: a covering player with K-T is advised
to raise to 90 to put the opponent all in, then judged `short.raise.small`,
whose explanation says to put them all in instead. The opponent's commitment
and the player's own all-in are not normalised consistently.

Needed: one decision identity and an immutable recommendation record,
including amount, matched commitment, purpose, assumptions and confidence.
Judge the chosen amount against that record and the same analysis. If analysis
is later revised, acknowledge the revision rather than blaming the player.

Evidence: `coach-brain.js` judgePreflop/advise/record;
`coach-talk.js` wordNow/verdictOf; `coach-lines.js` fold-win and short-stack lines.

### A02 — Advice can name an amount the controls cannot accept (high)

Reproduced: current wager 100, minimum raise increment 80, covering player
has 2,000 and opponent can match a total of 170. P.I.P. advises raise to
170; the real `wagerBounds()` requires 180. A covering player's raise must
remain legal even where part of it will be unmatched and returned.

The recent effective cap was applied independently of the engine's legal
wager bounds. Non-short preflop raises also still use their old uncapped size.

Needed: derive legal offered amounts through the engine's existing bounds,
and distinguish legal wager total from the amount actually matched.

Evidence: `coach-brain.js` raiseSize/postRaiseSize; `05-game-engine.js` wagerBounds/applyAction.

### A03 — Bluff and draw assessments do not fully judge the actual size (high)

Reproduced: an air bluff of 550 into 110 is classified `bet.bluff.good`,
with a required fold rate of 35%. That 35% belongs to P.I.P.'s suggested
60 bet; risking 550 needs about 83% folds for a pure bluff to break even.

Non-all-in draw bets likewise receive largely action-based assessments,
even if the player's amount consumes much more than the recommended bet.
Facing-bet value raises have no equivalent full size assessment.

Needed: use the actual incremental investment for every verdict. Evaluate
small/medium/large/effective-all-in draws and bluffs, not merely action labels.

Evidence: `coach-brain.js` judgeBet/foldChance/drawShove/judgePostflop.

### A04 — Identical preflop decisions can receive different advice (high)

Preflop equity is resampled separately while judging each candidate action.
Repeated advice also resamples; the eventual action record is judged again.
Postflop's WeakMap only caches a particular spot object, not an unchanged
game decision recreated by a tap or another update.

Reproduced unchanged Q-6 offsuit in the big blind against a button raise:
eight requests switched between call and fold, quoting estimates from 34%
to 38%. Close choices are legitimate; changing the answer without new
information makes the coach unreliable.

Needed: share one analysis per decision, cache by public decision state,
and express genuinely close choices as acceptable alternatives.

Evidence: `coach-brain.js` equityVs/postEquity/advise/record.

### A05 — Planned small commitments can be criticised as too small (medium)

Reproduced a set of Queens with 70 left and pot 1,000: advice all in 70,
purpose `commit-shallow`, but verdict `bet.value.small`/fine. The generic
small-bet threshold runs before the planned-commitment classification.

Needed: assess legal commitment and remaining-stack constraints before
ordinary pot-fraction sizing corrections.

### A06 — Folding for free can be praised as checking (medium)

`judgeBet()` normalises a fold into a check. Reproduced second pair,
no price to call: fold becomes `bet.check.medium`, good/clear. Normal controls
may prevent some such actions, but the brain's standalone assessment is wrong.

Needed: keep illegal or dominated actions distinct, and validate against
the engine's available actions before choosing advice.

### A07 — Several teaching lines overstate or contradict the strategy (high)

- `tip.the-basics`: "when it's free, always check" teaches never to value bet.
- `tip.raising-for-value`: broadly recommends raising two pair or better,
  regardless of the board and opponent's continuing range.
- `lesson.draw-shove` says to save all in for made strong hands, although
  the brain deliberately allows profitable draw shoves.
- A one-pair pot-control tip can conflict with profitable shallow-stack value.
- Some generic result lines praise play or claim nothing needs fixing when
  the coach simply has no selected verdict.
- "They checked, so probably weak" is too certain in situations where
  checking is normal, including acting first against the prior aggressor.
- `sizeWords()` can describe an over-pot bet as "all in" even when chips remain.

Needed: teach conditional principles, qualify opponent reads, and derive
the amount/commitment description from actual state. Perform a semantic
review of lessons and tips as well as the immediate recommendation lines.

Evidence: `coach-lines.js` tips/lessons/stories/summaries; `coach-brain.js` sizeWords.

## Tactical limits that need a deeper pass

### A08 — Value is estimated against the whole range, not the calling range

P.I.P. mostly asks whether the player beats likely opponent hands. To price
value correctly he must also ask which worse hands call this particular
amount, which better hands continue, and whether a raise is likely.
Current sizing fractions are useful defaults, not an optimisation.
The quiet-monster branch also precedes the caller adjustment, so its small
bet can override the opportunity to charge a habitual caller more.

Needed: size-conditioned continuation assumptions and target hands, with
explicit uncertainty. Include check as an option and avoid claiming a
precisely optimal amount where the opponent evidence is weak.

### A09 — Board strength, draws and position are too compressed

Kicker, nut-flush draw and backdoor information exist but are not used fully
by the coach's policy. Two pair, trips and other nominally strong categories
are grouped broadly. A single wetness score cannot capture every paired,
four-flush or highly connected board. Combo-draw outs differ between the
judgement and tap-read calculation; outs are not systematically discounted
for cards that improve the hand but still lose.

Calling prices use full-runout equity with a broad realisation adjustment.
A normal flop call usually buys the next card, not an assured free river.
Future prices, weak/non-nut draws and reverse implied odds need more explicit
treatment. Position and initiative should influence bet-versus-check plans,
not merely a small discount on calling equity.

### A10 — Bluffing and opponent modelling are coarse

Fold rates come from aggregated habits and a modest checking bonus, largely
independent of proposed size, street, board, effective stack and story.
After-the-flop range weights track action type but not wager size.
Two shown bluffs can label a player a bluffer without a corresponding
sample-confidence explanation. Individual mixed multiway opponents are
reduced to simple summaries. Card blockers and plausible bluff stories
are not used explicitly.

Needed: improve public-evidence modelling, confidence and size-sensitive
fold estimates; teach what stronger hands a bluff tries to fold and what
value hands the same betting sequence represents.

### A11 — Multiway pots and format-specific incentives are incomplete

One largest opponent stack represents effective commitment. Decisions use
the aggregate pot and equity against every opponent rather than separate
eligible main/side-pot equity. An all-in opponent can also remain in the
fold-chance calculation. Correct pot payout at showdown does not prove
correct pot-specific advice before it.

The brain stores mode but does not use it strategically: cash and tournament
advice do not account for different payout incentives. The draw-shove
penalty for risking an entire stack is a fixed heuristic, not a model of
tournament prizes. Format-sensitive coaching is future work; any Career
integration must first follow Career's own source-of-truth documents.

### A12 — Next-card planning is not durable or visible enough

`plan.next` is a useful guideline. P.I.P. does not carry a plan through
subsequent streets or compare planned pot growth. The decision record contains
spot/choice/judgement; it does retain next/purpose in judgement numbers for
value bets, but not the recommendation, full alternatives or their rationale.
Normal speech also does not expose the next-card guideline directly.

Needed: record what he recommended and why, what changed after the next
card/action, and the updated plan. Explain one relevant future branch rather
than giving the player a long decision tree.

## Teaching, interaction and lifecycle gaps

### A13 — The debrief is one selected decision, not a whole-hand explanation

The selection uses fixed severity/street/pot weights, not measured chip loss
or a causal analysis of the hand. Fold-win wording can be applied to an
earlier selected bet even when a later action actually caused the fold.
Generic repeated value-betting reminders can also discuss checking when
the actual issue was bet size.

Needed: distinguish outcome, the turning point, sound choices and one
improvement. Attribute the result to the relevant action; do not claim a
smaller bet would definitely have received a call with unseen cards.

### A14 — Timing and teaching completion need end-to-end checks

Delayed speech and queued messages have incomplete decision/hand identity
guards. Tap context uses hand/street/turn/result, not the actual wager or
decision revision. The immediate praise suppression compares action names,
not amount and normalised commitment; a wrong-sized raise can match and
the proper matched commitment can fail to match.

A vocabulary lesson is marked learned when its bubble starts appearing,
not when the player reads or understands it. Long combined story/advice/tail
lines can exceed the tested limits for individual authored lines.
The existing Hand Review setting also needs a deliberate relationship with
the proposed P.I.P. breakdown to avoid two competing teaching surfaces.

These are code-inspection risks; this audit did not perform an exhaustive
iPhone timing, interrupted-animation or screen-reader playthrough.

## What the present tests prove—and what they don't

- The existing suite was rerun: **59 brain checks passed**.
- It checks many random states, public-information boundaries, number sanity,
  familiar strategies and the presence/shape of authored lines.
- The audit's extra sandbox probes reproduced the failures above with the
  actual brain and, for the legal-amount case, the real engine wager bounds.
- Random advice checks reject advice labelled a **clear** mistake, but can
  allow advice labelled a leaning mistake. One probe found a short-opponent
  capped value bet recommended and simultaneously labelled `bet.value.shove`
  as a mistake. Strengthening the assertion matters.
- Existing action fixtures simplify the engine and do not prove full legal
  sizing, reopening rules, speech routing or per-hand recommendation continuity.
- Tests using the brain's own judge as the strategy oracle cannot establish
  poker quality by themselves. Independently reasoned teaching examples and
  complete-hand playthroughs are needed.
- Phone responsiveness needs measurement. The coach calls synchronous
  sampling rather than the existing worker service; taps/updates can repeat it.

## Proposed project plan

### Phase 1 — Reliable decisions and honest language

**Implemented in v0.58.3-dev.** See the completion entry in
[BRAIN_PLAN.md](BRAIN_PLAN.md). The findings above remain the historical audit
of v0.58.2; the Phase 1 regressions now cover their reproduced reliability
failures. Strategic limitations listed under Phase 2 are not claimed fixed.

- Use shared legal actions and wager bounds, preserving real poker rules.
- Store a recommendation and shared analysis per decision, including the
  exact advised amount and effective commitment.
- Make advice, actual-action assessment, taps and result explanations agree.
- Correct all-in/matched-stack/tiny-commitment classifications.
- Assess actual bluff/draw/raise investment; reject free folds appropriately.
- Review every lesson/tip for misleading absolutes and conditional exceptions.
- Guard deferred speech against changed hands, cards, prices and decisions.
- Add regression scenarios for every reproduced issue, plus rapid actions,
  re-raises, capped wagers and unchanged repeated requests.
- Acceptance: exact advice never receives an unexplained contradictory
  assessment; recommended amounts are legal; analysis does not change without
  new information; unknown cards never become known facts in speech.

### Phase 2 — Deeper tactical decisions

- Evaluate practical bet/raise/check alternatives against likely continuation
  ranges; state which weaker hands a value bet targets.
- Improve kicker, nut potential, board-relative strength and draw quality.
- Distinguish next-card price from full-runout odds and future betting risk.
- Improve position, initiative, trapping and thin-value decisions.
- Make bluff selection and sizing sensitive to board, opponent evidence,
  blockers and the story represented by previous bets.
- Separate main-pot and side-pot incentives; exclude all-in opponents from
  foldable-opponent assumptions.
- Carry a simple plan across streets and explain why new information changes it.
- Assess whether format-specific incentives are needed for the available games.
- Acceptance: a curated scenario matrix independently checks action, amount,
  rationale, uncertainty and continuation plan across contrasting situations.

### Phase 3 — P.I.P. HAND BREAKDOWN

- Present a short breakdown once the result is known and before the next deal.
- Cover what happened, one choice that worked, one improvement where supported,
  and one takeaway. Allow "no clear mistake" rather than inventing criticism.
- Let the player continue immediately or expand/tap for more detail.
- Keep decisions judged by information available at the time, with later
  revealed hands identified as later evidence.
- Coordinate with Auto-Continue, TALK preferences and existing Hand Review.
- Use a machine-native review surface consistent with the Table's art direction.
- Acceptance: correct attribution, no result-based blame, readable on phone,
  no forced long lecture and no verdict spilling into the next hand.

### Phase 4 — Practice rooms and targeted lessons

- Build small scenario lessons for value extraction, folding, price/odds,
  bluffing, board changes and opponent reads.
- Ask the player to choose a move and amount before P.I.P. reveals his answer.
- Explain acceptable alternatives and let the player replay contrasting cases.
- Offer "why this amount?", "what if they raise?" and "why check?" prompts.
- Teach decision habits using the same corrected brain as live play.
- Acceptance: practice outcomes assess reasoning, not merely whether a random
  showdown was won; lessons explain exceptions and build understanding.

### Phase 5 — Persistent coaching observations

- Track a few supported habits with enough eligible opportunities to assess them.
- Separate situational mistakes from repeated patterns and respect uncertainty.
- Present observations in the owner's distinct **P.I.P. NOTICED** format.
- Link each observation to specific hand evidence and an appropriate lesson.
- Let the player inspect, reset or disable coaching memory without changing
  game settings, saves or lifetime statistics.
- Measure improvement and show restrained session recaps where useful.
- Acceptance: observations are correct, explainable, infrequent and helpful;
  displaying a lesson is never treated as proof of mastery.

### Later optional features

- Revisit a recent hand with a street-by-street replay and saved recommendation.
- "Explain the number" views for pot odds, draws and estimate uncertainty.
- Player-controlled learning depth; optional glossary and lesson revision.
- Session recap focused on one or two priorities rather than a wall of scores.
- A diagnostic case export with public state, chosen action and spoken wording
  to make future play reports easy to reproduce.
- Performance work using cached analysis and, if measured necessary, the
  existing worker machinery; no new dependency is implied.

## Poker references used for the assessment

These support strategic principles, not claims that a particular fixed
fraction is universally optimal:

- [PokerStars Learn: Value Betting](https://www.pokerstars.com/poker/learn/lesson/value-betting/)
  — value depends on worse hands continuing, and size affects that range.
- [PokerStars Learn: Stack-to-Pot Ratio](https://www.pokerstars.com/poker/learn/lesson/introduction-to-stack-to-pot-ratio-spr/)
  — remaining effective chips and pot size influence commitment decisions.
- [PokerStars Learn: Implied Odds](https://www.pokerstars.com/poker/learn/strategies/calculating-implied-odds-and-when-it-matters/)
  — future payments, position and potentially losing after improvement matter.

## Recommended next task

Start Phase 1 with shared decision analysis and legal sizing, then use that
same record to align advice, chosen-action feedback and the result. Correct
actual-size assessments and misleading lesson lines before expanding tactics.
This is a proposed reprioritisation based on the audit, not a completed build.
