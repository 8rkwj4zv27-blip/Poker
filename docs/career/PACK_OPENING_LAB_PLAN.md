# Poker Faces — Pack Opening Lab brief

2026-10-10. Owner-authorised isolated presentation study. Pass 1 was rejected by
its owner: its successful functional checks did not establish tactile or creative
quality. This brief supersedes the automated card flips, optical reader, chip
sample and cabinet swatches in the original proposal. Implementation and critical
review are recorded in `PACK_OPENING_LAB_PASS_2.md`; Pass 1 remains historical.

## Direction and scope

Make opening a packet a playable moment within the strange velvet-covered poker
machine. Green felt provides a spacious handling surface; burgundy, pixel print,
cream stock, chunky shadows and mustard controls carry the established character.
Objects should be enjoyable to handle before any reward is announced.

Choose one of three packets, pick it up, inspect both sides, tear its seam, pull
out its contents, handle each find, inspect event information and keep the haul.
Buying, odds, inventory, duplicates, ownership and event redemption are outside
this lab. Preserve all 16 approved invitation faces and the separate Card Visual
Lab. Use current gameplay chip sprites and the current Classic card back.

## Continuous objects

Each packet, card, torn strip and loose chip is one persistent object. Cards
begin inside a clipped pocket between the packet's rear lining and front foil.
The front occludes their lower sections during extraction. When they clear the
mouth, transfer those same objects to the felt at their measured positions.

Handling follows the finger immediately. Weight appears through tilt, contact
shadows, stock thickness, foil response, settling and sparse sound. This is a
controlled 2.5D illusion, not a general rigid-body simulator. Buttons provide
equivalent actions without requiring precision gestures.

1. **Choose and handle.** Three authored House/Club/Reserve foil packets share a
   believable construction. Pick up, move, turn and return a sealed packet.
   Series prices are illustrative labels, with no transaction.
2. **Tear.** A broad seam gesture advances a stepped, jagged tear along the hand's
   path. Damage is irreversible; releasing a partial tear leaves a curled strip
   to resume. The separated strip lands on the same felt. An OPEN SEAM key gives
   the equivalent completed action. Top crimping must remain visible under tilt.
3. **Draw.** Pull the exposed stack upward. It remains occluded by the front foil
   until it clears the mouth. A cancelled pull returns the stack into the packet.
   The empty wrapper collapses to the side and stays movable.
4. **Handle and discover.** The stack is face-up. A short drag peeks without
   advancing; a decisive flick places the current find in the received fan and
   exposes the next. No automatic flip, disappearing card or fly-off-screen
   transition. Exposed stack edges and FAN STACK support a second way to browse.
5. **Inspect.** Lift the same object on the same table. Event name, prize and
   rarity sit above it; event terms and special-format synopsis sit below it.
   Drag turns the whole object, including its stock edge and reverse. Manual
   turn/front keys remain available. Returning restores its handling position.
   There is no separate reader appliance or event brief printed on the reverse.
6. **Keep.** Previously received finds stay reachable. End with a spread or stack
   of the actual haul. KEEP gathers those objects; no intervening victory page.

## Rarity and event personality

Rarity must be legible in the static card first. Retain the approved paper,
colour-print, expanded Rare and bespoke full-face Legendary editions. A finite
material catch, light echo and audio accent accompany first discovery; controls
remain available. Revisits do not replay a new-find ceremony.

Table catches warm casino light. Bomb has genuinely missing edges and detached
hot embers. Wild has displaced print and a tilt-responsive expression. Freezeout
has cold reflective print, with finite cold motes reserved for premium finds.
Effects must follow the handled card and rest afterwards. With motion and sound
disabled, the object itself must still carry the rarity upgrade.

Sound follows contact: foil rustle, progress-linked granular tear, paper slide,
landing, ceramic chip clacks and restrained rarity accents. Start from user
interaction, offer independent mute and cancel stale sounds on reset.

## Extra finds

Four invitations plus a small foil sleeve of house chips provide five finds.
The sleeve tips and a chunk of actual current gameplay chips falls from its
mouth. Use the seven current decorative colours and three sprite variants;
colour does not invent denominations. Chips bounce, overlap into a small pile,
clack and remain movable. A four-drum amount readout acknowledges the fixture
reward without writing a bankroll.

An optional sixth find is the actual Classic card-back print with a small paper
band. Its inspection explains that it is a visual sample. No cabinet swatches,
invented chip artwork, ownership writes or new currencies.

Order includes early and late special finds rather than always building to one

## Quality gates

- Judge recorded movement as well as stills: tear-to-mouth, extraction-to-hand,
  peek-to-flick, chip emergence and inspect-to-return must remain connected.
- Ordinary cards and muted handling must be pleasurable; effects cannot rescue
  weak motion, artwork or object geometry.
- Inspect all 16 event/rarity pairs at phone size. Preserve title/prize styling
  and readable event information. Bomb/Wild conditions stay under review.
- Check one persistent object through pocket, scene, inspection and received
  pile. Partial tears, cancelled draws, cancelled drags and reset must not
  duplicate, lose or unexpectedly advance contents.
- Review narrow/short phones and desktop; touch, mouse, keyboard, mute and
  Reduced Motion; avoid overflow and unreachable main controls.
- Use current gameplay chips/back. Check no storage writes and run focused
  Career regressions. Add no framework or dependency and change no poker logic.
- Correct concrete visual shortcomings before delivery. Passing checks is not
  owner approval. Actual iPhone timing, touch latency, audio and refresh-rate
  feel remain a separate review; browser emulation cannot establish them.
